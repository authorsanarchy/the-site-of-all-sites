The War With No One



Welcome to the performance. Tonight I come to regale a tale from days of old.
Our tale tonight is from Northern lands of frost and bitter cold.
Of grudges foretold, our story unfolds of wolves and peaks and ancient holds.
A tale of the bold and the last one told by a place now forgotten to time.
A fateful climb in the winter’s rime marks the last page of a quarrel from a different age.

Now enough of my amble, I do love to remble, let me read you the story as its wrote:

They were hiding in the mountains.
That much we knew.
Cowering in their craggy caves, plotting for our doom.
We knew we could not let this stand.
We could not let them force our hand.
Our endless feud would soon be no more. Yes, we would put an end to our drawn out war and bring it all to their front door!

Long had we done battle with our evilest of foes. Since the founding of our village, they’d say. The vilest of villains, most deceitful of demons, barbarians impossible to tame.
They’d strike at us quickly without warning or fear, hounding us like deer in the night. It had been many a year since we last did sight but we knew they always hid near. No longer could we wait for their foulness to appear, our days of waiting had long come to pass.
We would rally our strength, scale to their home and bring an end to them at long last.

A simple challenge lay before us on our march to bring doom, the wintery woods before the great grey slopes. Woods we knew well, so we thought. Yes, wind and wolves and wargs and wights would stalk within the wild woodland but we? We had might. We knew our cause was right and we would not be stopped by any blight.
So in we marched with spirits high like a tide of shining steel. The woods were dark and heavy with thorns. We lost two, then ten, then twenty men to the dark, to never see the other side. It happened so swiftly we hadn’t time to mourn for we knew at once we’d been found! Our enemy must have known of our plot. In these shadowglossed woods they would strike us down in the way of the hound, from the shadows, always unseen. We’d run and we’d hide in a forest filled with screams of kin dragged to a swift bloody grave.
We escaped to the slope of the mountain with all that we could save. Quickly counting the fallen before choosing what would be our way. We had lost much of our force to an enemy still unseen and yet returning through the woods would surely lead to more’s demise.
We all knew there was but one choice, we would have to remain brave. Keep our quest to find their home, face the mountains and rise!

We thundered up the splintered slopes with vigour, vengeance and spite. Though wind and hail and snow fought back they could not halt us from our attack as we clawed with raw and reddening hands up the slopes unhindered by nature's demands. Yet there from the very peak of the height like a wrath from the divine with intent to smite came a murderous blanket of white. A pelting of rocks and snow dealt many around us a sundering blow as we held for dear life to our course.
Of course our enemy must have once again known the where and the how of how to wound us. The maleficent bastards once again got their way as our comrades fell from the cliff in droves to be buried and crushed under the vilest of blankets of snow. But they did not slay us all upon that day. I and many others did yet survive and wounded an haggard as we were there was only one thing to say: that we would make them pay!

We knew they must have thought that we’d all had died for our ascent from there was unhindered. We reached the very crest of the greatest of hills, minds filled with murderous thrills as we found the mouth to their cave. We were bruised, starved and parched and yet as we marched to the dark we knew our enemy waited within. The march within the cave was long and cold; we went for many days with naught to eat or drink but cave water and mold. The deeper we went the more of us got sick, succumbing to the illness of the mold; surely another of our enemies' vile tricks.
When our numbers had lowered to that of only five, we all were fraught with doubt if indeed our enemy dwelled in a place so deep. Yet soon we all saw something that made us once again alive, light at the end of the tunnel, and clearly carved stone as we knew we’d found our enemies secret home. We had no choice but to finish with our quest, we could not turn back, best we’d hoped for a warrior's death. To bring blood to them as they’d brought blood to us as the first of our people to have ever reached their heart with little strength but our bonds of trust we charged into the blinding lights ahead.

The sight that lay before us once the blindness shrieked from our eyes was not one that was easy to describe. Where we’d each expected a glorious brawl to the end instead what we saw filled us each with great dread, as a great stone city on the mountain's edge sat empty. And dead.
At first we all thought it must be a trick, that they hid from us to strike again unseen. And yet the snow was fresh, and as we furthered into their lands we never met with the sting of death as each building and stall we checked was clean.
We were certain they had nowhere else they could hide, we’d have long since found any other vile nest and yet this place was empty with naught even a single chest.
We knew they had been there; in the forest; atop the slopes. They’d tricked us with their poisoned mold and led us to our deaths out in the icy cold. They’d sicced us with their woodland beasts and buried us in the snow. They must be dwelling somewhere. They must be dwelling here, yet plain for all to see they were not.
Where could they be, if not here?

One answer could be so clear.

Had we not seen any in many a year because it had been many a year since any had dwelled here?
Had the wild wolves weed fought been simply wild and foul. Had the snow that came upon us just been nature’s violent cowl. Had all our lost kin simply fallen to the night, and our struggles against this enemy been a vain and meaningless fight?
But our war and our feud! It could not end in such disgrace. They had plagued us for years, that was certain and known. We had to stake our revenge and slay them all within their home. They could not leave and deny us our vengeful rights!
This crusade came at too great a cost that we couldn’t see the truth through all the loss.

That we had gone to war with no one.

And lost.
