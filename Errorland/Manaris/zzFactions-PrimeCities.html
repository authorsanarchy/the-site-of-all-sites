The Six Holy Cities of the Prime Gods



Known colloquially as the Prime Cities, the origins of the Six Holy Cities of the Prime Gods can
be traced back the year 124 after union. As part of the wider colonial efforts by humans during
the Age of Smoke, predominantly Merin settlers arrived on the continent of Rinhar, making
landfall first on the Eastern coast, swiftly founding the city of Enora.


In the decades that followed, following numerous conflicts with the indigenous populations of
Rinhar, the settlers largely converted to the Prime Faith—the dominant religion among the native
population on the continent. Nominally, the widespread conversion was meant as a show of
friendship between the settler and indigenous population, though numerous scholars contest that
it was done largely as a means of integrating their own beliefs into the religion as a means of
controlling the native populations. Whatever the intent, the settlers renamed the cities
of Qaita, Unaya, Hastila and Marania in honour of deities of the Prime Faith which would be joined
by the cities of Enora and Vordanya in a formal political union in the year 204 after union. The
union would serve as the only major nation on the continent of Rinhar, home to a population of
around 500,000 at its peak.


The terms of the union between the cites—which remained largely consistent throughout its
existence—provided each city with a right to self-goverence within borders outlined by the
Treaty of Enora. The cities were to remain closely tied both economically and politically,
operating tax free trade within their borders and hosting regular diplomatic congregations to
manage affairs and settle disputes. Most importantly, in times of conflict, a declaration of war by
or against any of the Prime Cities bound all of the cities to come to their aid. The clause of
military cooperation was called upon a number of times throughout the duration of the union,
most notably during the War of the Alliances—a conflict initially sparked by a formal
declaration of war by the city of Unaya.


While the union never formally changed its name from the Six Holy Cities of the Prime Gods, its
membership diminished over the course of its existence.


Following the Great Revolt of 977, Marania distanced itself from the rest of the alliance in
objection to the treatment of rebels in other cities. While Marania never formally withdrew from
the union, the city almost never sent diplomats to formal congregations, formed trade
partnerships with states sanctioned by the other cities and refused to send soldiers to fight in wars
that didn’t actively threaten the safety of the other cities.


Following the Fall of Vordanya in 1481—a series of violent tragedies within the city that
ultimately lead to its utter destruction—the borders from the initial Treaty of Enora were
redrawn, with the majority of land once bequeathed to Vordnaya being divided between the cities
of Enora and Qaita, leaving only a small, mostly ceremonial patch of unclaimed land
surrounding where the city had once stood, and the now extinct city continued to be named
among the Six, if only as a formality. Many among the Brotherhood of Slayers often suspected
that part of the reason the lands surrounding Vordanya remained unclaimed was simply because
neither the city of Enora nor Qaita wished to be held responsible for dealing with the undead
which frequented Vordanya’s ruin.


While the union of the Prime Cities was never formally ended, by the year of the Sundering it
had been effectively rendered obsolete. The cities of both Hastila and Marania were utterly
destroyed in the year 4364 by forces still largely unknown. From 4112-4413 the city of Qaita
suffered sieges from a variety of foes, drastically reducing its population until the city was
eventually sacked by orcish raiders. While the cities of Enora and Unaya both remain standing to
this day, both were abandoned by their human populations before the Sundering, only to later be
resettled without any of their former political ties by populations predominantly from other
species—elves in the case of Unaya and Gnomes in the case of Enora.
