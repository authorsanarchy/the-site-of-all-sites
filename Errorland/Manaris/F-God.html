The God and the King



Hear thee a tale of a mortal man, hailed through the realm King.
So raised by his greatness and praise was he, as to think of himself worthy of divinity.
Hear thee a tale of an undying God, whose name, through the land was revered.
Of a power to be both respected and feared, none would dare claim equal to he.

Hear thee a tale of ruin wrought by a feud of an untold scale.

Hear thee a tale of the battles fought when the God and the King went to war.

Our tale begins on a perfect morning, beneath a cloudless vibrant blue sky. The grass was green, the birds sang in a beautiful key, and the sun cast down many a warming ray.
The people mingled happily on with their day, for the weather and the peace, things for which they would pray. All but the King in his castle above. In his empty chamber, on his lonely throne, he sat, and he wondered:
"Why?"
He had plenty of wealth in his money, his jewels and his many golden rings. He had respect too, all would wish him good health. And yet despite all his praise, when times became tough and the people were afraid, it was not to him, but to the God that they prayed.
The God that they trusted.
The God that they loved more than he.
In his moments alone, at the peak of his glower, he would think to himself no God deserves such power. He’d say, “I am their master, their leader and King! It is I who they should be out there worshipping.”
And so, on this one rather fateful day, the King decided that he had a visit to pay, to the old witch who dwelled in the ditch, and the tomes she kept with her in her home.
Her house was rickety and covered with mold. Her cauldron sat in the centre and bubbled, the place where she toiled and reaped from its troubles. When she looked at the King, her eyes were cold as he gave her his decree that he sought to attain divinity.
She looked for a while into her simmering stew, before telling the King what it was, he must do. “Hidden in the depths of this world is a scroll, holding many secrets you seek. If this is your path, then you may claim it indeed, but know it extracts a heavy toll. Should you seek to ascend to the divine, know that ruin may be the only thing you find.”
Spurred by these words and undeterred by their warning, the King summoned his bravest of men. He sent them forth to scour the lands, to retrieve him this scroll from wherever it lay. But the King was not the only one watching that day, and to those who might usurp him? Well, to them, the God had something to say.
The knights the King sent fell each in their turn, to magic or monsters or worse, till but one stood strong while his fellows did burn, vainly fighting the heavenly curse. As word reached the King of the fall of his men, he realised such events were not unexplained and that the God had took offence to his search. The King shouted to the heavens, “I challenge thee. I do not fear you in your false divinity. Leave your lofty perch. Come down to face me!”
The prideful type, the God complied, stepping down from the heavens above. He shook rivers and mountains with every stride, till he stood before the King and bellowed aloud, “You think yourself my better? I think you’re far too proud.”
Across the world, the last of the knights dove to darkened depths for his King. He had found himself a desolate rocky cove when a shimmer had caught the corner of his eye. At the bottom of a black and murky lagoon sat a case for a scroll made purely from gold. With no other choice than to do as he was told, our knight plunged into the dim dingy deep.
Back in the kingdom a battle was brewing, when the King struck out at the God. He raised his blade and called to his army in crusade, and to bring him the head of the God. From the heavens above, fire rained. Buildings were ruined. Much was lost for so little was gained. The armies were sundered, and the soldiers ran in fear, but the King never faltered, bidding his foe to come near as they finished their battle man to God. For hours they fought, as they struggled and cried. For hours they struggled, until finally…
the God died. After a very long journey, the final knight stumbled home, to be faced by a ghastly sight. His town, his home, was little more than a pyre, all buildings but one reduced to soot. As he wandered through the wreckage to himself, he did cry, “Oh what happened here? What started this fire?”
From the last standing structure an answerer came. The witch from before, wandered out of her home and stared at the knight with her stone-cold eye. In her raspy voice, she said, “I’ll tell you dear squire. Your god and your king went to war.”
All else in the kingdom had fled or joined the ash. All else in the kingdom save for one. The once grand castle was little more than stones, and the royals who lived there were nothing but bones, yet there in his chamber sat the King, on a still smouldering throne.
The knight could only stare at his miserable King on his ravaged throne with his melting rings. His heart broke, filled with regret and shame over his once great home and all the ruin for which he felt he was to blame. He reached from his bag and tore the scroll in twain, as he looked up at his King and loudly proclaimed, “Long shall be your reign. The God and the King of nothing.”
