The Planet of Manaris

A Note:
Much to the chagrin of many, the term “Manaris” is commonly used both to refer to the
planet of Manaris as well as the realms as a whole, often with little distinction between. In an
effort to avoid confusion, the “planet of Manaris” will be used to refer to the planet located
within the Mortal Realm, while the term “Manaris” will be used to refer to the realms as a whole.

Meaning “Home of Magic” in the old draconic speech, the name Manaris first emerged
during the Age of Dragons, to refer to the planet upon which they resided shortly after early
astronomers deduced the existence of other celestial bodies in the skies far above. The only
known planet in the Mortal Realm, and the only known location in all the realms to be comprised
of all four core elements of water, earth, fire and air, the planet of Manaris is believed by most to
exist at the nexus point where the four elemental realms collide, making it by most
approximations the center of the known universe, as well as the birthplace of all life.


Composition of the planet:
The planet is geologically composed of five layers.

The Core composes the very center of the planet, of unknown composition, theorized to be
composed of either pure elemental fire, pure elemental light or pure elemental shadow, although
no expeditions have ever reached the core and returned with their findings.

The Median surrounds the core and is made up of magma formed from a mixture of elemental earth and fire. Very little has been done to study or map the region given its inhospitability.

The Surface is composed mostly of elemental earth and water, making up the continents and
seas where most of the planet’s life dwells.

The Skies is composed of elemental water and air, forming the breathable atmosphere to
sustain the life found upon the surface.

The Frimament is a very thin bubble of pure elemental life which surrounds the planet,
protecting it from the elemental shadow of the Void which surrounds the planet.


The planet of Manaris had been accurately mapped for many millennia, first by the early
draconic civilizations who quickly explored and spread across every corner of their world.
Maps have traditionally divided the planets surface into the ten continents of
Acual, Dirvat, Thaenor, Sai Palan, Merinban, Sigal, Saurin, Rinhar, Kafal Bir and Mavanir,
as well as the three oceans of Freyas, Koramirth and Abanis.


Following the Sundering, the surface of Manaris saw many great changes as continents

were ripped apart by earthquakes, drowned beneath floods and buried under volcanic ash. Thus
far, explorers have yet to produce a complete map of the world following the sundering.
