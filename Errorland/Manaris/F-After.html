Happily Ever After



“…And they all lived happily ever after.”
Or so the tale goes. When we reach the end of the storied prose, a princess was saved when the hero arose to become a new king with the old tyrant deposed, the book is to be closed. There’s no more story to compose because they all lived happily ever after. Right?
What of the hero, born in a barn? His life lived on a farm, never wanting to do harm. Left home on a dare from a talking bear with his trusty mare on a fools quest to snare a rare golden hare. He never asked to stumble into the bandit’s old cave, but then he saved the old man he thought was a slave who told him to be brave, that there was a princess to be saved if he would sail across the waves and take the old tyrant to his grave. Now he himself was king, and it didn’t take him long to find that he didn’t know a thing. In barely any time he’d drained the coffers of almost every dime. When illness spread across the swine and there was no gold left in any mine he found he simply had no spine and drowned himself in wine.
What of the princess too long trapped in a tower? Her despot of a father wouldn’t let anything touch his flower. In every lonely hour she’d dream what she'd do with his kind of power, or at the very least a warm shower yet all she had were cold stone walls and her thoughts growing ever dour. So when the hero came to save her, she owed him, she supposed, and she accepted two days later when he suddenly proposed. She soon, though, found that she could never truly come to hold him dear, that quickly became very clear. She mentioned leaving and it brought him nearly to tears and so to their marriage she adhered for far too many a year. Then the children came that she had to raise, she thought maybe for them she could convince herself to stay, yet she’d had no mother herself to teach her what to say and so often she found them starting to stray. She felt like she was losing the way and decided that she simply couldn’t take it one day and left that night without a word.
What of the tyrant of old? His body now cold, fast growing mold. As a boy all he’d ever known was a throne that he’d inherit once he’d grown. Raised with no mother, fearing the plots of his brother, he’d had his wits alone to not join his father’s bones, he started to fear the castle’s marbled stone. With a youth ever wrought by strife he thought perhaps a peaceful life could come the day he chose a wife. Yet she seemed ever distant and though his love was persistent she remained ever resistant, insistent it wouldn’t work to the day she simply walked away. Now he had a daughter to raise but he never had the time, there was that business with the mine and they had to inoculate the swine. He did his best not to fall into the sweet embrace of wine but one can only hold out for so long. He didn’t set out to be cruel, but perhaps that is simply the nature of rule, as his mind, like thread, began to unspool, the people called him a ghoul, cheering as their new hero cut him down.
Happily ever after indeed.
I suppose this is all here to say, that perhaps think twice—even if they seem nice—before committing to someone you’ve just met. Please beware of the dares of talking bears, they seldom have your best interests at heart. And perhaps next time we crown a new king, maybe, just maybe we don’t choose the last one's offspring, or worse the fool that killed him. I’ve grown rather tired of telling over and over the same old rhyme. We all know where it starts, we all know where it ends, we only just wrapped up the last one, I don’t want to start yet another “Once upon a time…”
